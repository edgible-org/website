The world we're entering

AI is about to know everything about us.

Soon each of us will have AI agents working on our behalf: booking, writing, remembering, deciding. They'll build small apps on the fly, for one family, one club, one firm, and throw most of them away.

They only work well when they know us: our files, our habits, our clients, our children. It will be the most personal computing has ever been.

The easy place to run all of it is a big platform's cloud, and the price is quiet but enormous: every agent's memory, every app's records and every question we ask becomes data held, and often used, by someone else.

It doesn't have to be that way.

Why it matters

It's your data. Keep it.

Models will change. Agents will change. Platforms will come and go. What lasts is the data: your members, your clients, your family's photos. And every new AI makes it worth more. Edgible exists to give you the confidence to keep it close.

Your data is the value

Whoever holds your data holds the value. It should be you, not the platform you happen to use this year.

It belongs close to you

On a computer at home or in the office, or a small server of your own, where you and your AI can reach it from anywhere.

Trust should come with proof

Not a promise in the terms and conditions, but evidence you can see: where your data is, and that it's safe.

What we do today

Your AI sets it up. We keep it safe.

One example, start to finish

  1. You ask

    "It costs us $40 a month to host our small website. Is there a better way?"

  2. Your agent asks first

    Is it a few simple pages, or something you edit every week? Then it lays out two or three honest options, including free hosting if that really is better, and recommends one. It picks from recipes we've tested, or helps you find a new one.

  3. It sets it up on a machine you control

    A computer you already have, or a small server of your own for a few dollars a month. Your site gets your own address and a padlock, the editor sits behind your sign-in, and nobody gets into your network.

  4. Edgible looks after it

    The recipe is tested on real machines every week. Backups count only once they've been restored and checked. If you change machines, your site moves with you.

Same website. A fraction of the cost. And it's yours.

Private or public

Where is the padlock opened?

Every private website uses a padlock to encrypt what travels between a visitor and the site. Most hosting and tunnel services open that padlock on their own servers, handle the traffic, then pass it on. With Edgible, the padlock stays locked all the way to your machine.

Keeping it private?

Member lists, client files, an AI that reads your documents: keep the padlock on your machine, and use Edgible.

Running something public?

Edgible still works, and so do other products, such as Cloudflare. Pick what suits you.

Which AI reads it?

You choose: a model running on your own machine, or a service you pick. Edgible doesn't choose for you.

Before you start

What you need, what it costs, who sets it up.

What you need

A computer that stays on, or a small server of your own for a few dollars a month. If it's off, your apps are off until it's back. And an AI assistant that works on your computer, such as Claude Code or Codex. A chat app on its own can't.

What it costs

Edgible is in beta. Pricing will be freemium: there will always be a free tier, and paid tiers such as Pro and Enterprise. Prices will be released soon, and will be competitive.

Where your backups live

Where you choose, on your own machines. Edgible never holds a copy. We suggest a second copy somewhere else.

Who sets it up

Your AI does, a step at a time, and asks before anything changes. For a club or a firm, a volunteer or your IT person can do it once.

When it isn't the right fit: a simple website (free hosts are easier), heavy public traffic or video, and regulated records at scale, which need compliant hosting and professional advice.
